## The measured window, and the correction that matters most

| | first round (nominal) | measured |
|---|---|---|
| start | 1 Feb 2020 | 2020-02-01 00:00:00 |
| end | 31 Aug 2020 | **2020-09-01 03:59:50** |
| calendar span | 212 d | **213.17 d** |
| **logged time** | not considered | **175.27 d** |

**331 gaps longer than 60 s remove 37.90 days, 17.8 % of the calendar.** The first
round used calendar time as exposure. That inflated the MTBF by 21.6 %, and it
inflated it in the flattering direction, which is the pattern CLAUDE.md says to hunt
for first.

| exposure | MTBF | 90 % interval |
|---|---|---|
| calendar, 213.2 d | 53.3 d | 23.3 to 156.0 d |
| **logged, 175.3 d** | **43.8 d** | **19.1 to 128.3 d** |

Logged time is a proxy for operating time, not a measurement of it. The evidence that
it is the better proxy: most gaps fall overnight (01:19 to 04:36, 00:43 to 04:38, and
so on), consistent with a parked train that is neither running nor logging. And one
gap is unambiguous, a 21.5 h hole from 07/06 14:19 to 08/06 11:48, which starts within
minutes of failure #3 ending at 14:30 and closes just before its maintenance at 16:00.
That gap is the train out of service.

Neither denominator is certain, so **both stay published**. The honest statement is
that the MTBF is between roughly 44 and 53 days, with a 90 % interval that spans at
least 19 to 156 days. The interval is the answer; the point estimate is not.

The trend conclusion is unchanged and robust: **Laplace U = +0.50**, far inside
+/- 1.96. No evidence of deterioration and none of improvement. A homogeneous Poisson
process stands, and anyone fitting a beta to four points is reading noise.

Sampling coverage during the failure windows is good, so the events themselves are
observable: 100 %, 101 %, 92 % and 100 % of expected samples.

## Correction 1: the rate. My first-round correction was itself wrong

The first round said the README's "1 Hz" was wrong and the rate is 0.1 Hz. The
arithmetic was right and the conclusion was not.

The index column runs 0 to 15,169,470 in steps of **exactly 10**, every row. The
primary source says 15,169,480 points at 1 Hz. So the APU was **acquired at 1 Hz** and
the **published file is decimated 10 to 1**, giving 0.1 Hz on disk. Both figures are
correct, at different levels, and the UCI page and the PDF are each quoting a different
one.

Timestamp steps confirm it and add nuance: 88.2 % are exactly 10 s, 8.5 % are 9 s,
2.5 % are 12 s. The clock is not exact, so any rate computed per sample rather than per
elapsed second will drift.

What I did wrong: I found a real inconsistency, resolved it against one source, and
wrote "wrong" where the evidence only supported "these two numbers measure different
things". A discrepancy between two sources is not proof that one is false.

## Correction 2: H1 is not what the graph says

The topology was built from the 2022 paper, which describes H1 as a valve activated
when the command pressure switch reads above 10.2 bar. **The primary source for our
dataset says something else**: H1 is the pressure generated by the pressure drop when
the **cyclonic separator filter** discharges.

So the graph had H1 mounted on the wrong component, carried a threshold that does not
belong to it, and was missing a piece of equipment entirely. This is the risk the
topology document itself warned about, and it landed anyway: sensor definitions do not
carry over between MetroPT variants as cleanly as the shared tag names suggest.

The 8.2 bar (MPG) and 7 bar (LPS) thresholds do appear in the primary source and
survive. Motor current gains a fourth level the 2022 paper omits: **9 A at start-up**,
alongside 0 A off, 4 A offloaded and 7 A under load.

## Correction 3: there are no orphan tags

The first round reported that three of the fifteen tags had no published description,
and marked them inferred in the graph. The primary source describes **all fifteen**:

- **Reservoirs** (bar): downstream pressure of the reservoirs, which should sit close
  to the pneumatic panel pressure TP3. That relationship is itself a testable
  redundancy check.
- **Pressure Switch**: detects the discharge in the air-drying towers.
- **Caudal Impulse**: counts the pulse outputs generated by the absolute amount of air
  flowing from the APU to the reservoirs.

The lesson is narrow and worth keeping: the gap existed because the dataset page and
the sibling paper were treated as the source when the actual source was inside the
download.
